The water cycle describes how water moves continuously between the Earth and the atmosphere.
Heat from the sun causes water in oceans, lakes and rivers to evaporate and rise as vapour.
As the vapour rises it cools down and condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds.
When the droplets join together and become heavy, they fall back to the ground as rain, snow or hail.
This process is called precipitation. Some of the water flows over the land as runoff and returns to the oceans,
while some of it soaks into the soil and becomes groundwater. Plants also release water vapour through their leaves,
a process known as transpiration. The water cycle is essential for life because it supplies fresh water to every living thing.
